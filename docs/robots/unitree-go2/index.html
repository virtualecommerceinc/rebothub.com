Unitree Go2

The most widely owned legged robot outside a lab. Its used market is where the used-humanoid market will be rehearsed first: licence tiers, firmware, and actuator wear decide value more than cosmetics.

Verdict key: Fix when the part costs clearly less than the robot's used value and the job is routine. Sell for parts when the fault is a board, camera or sensor on a robot whose used value is low. Replace when the robot is old enough that even a successful repair leaves you with something not worth much. Get a price first when it depends on a quote or the robot's used value. Part cost is a tier, not a quote; prices change and we do not print them.

Support noteGo2 trims (Air, Pro, EDU) differ in what the owner is allowed to do with the software. A used Go2 is only worth the EDU premium if the EDU licence demonstrably transfers. Ask Unitree before paying for it. Official support.

Buying used

Inspect it in ten minutes

  • Ask for the exact trim (Air, Pro, EDU) and proof of what the software licence allows a second owner to do.
  • Watch it stand, walk, and recover from a push; listen for clicking from any leg.
  • Ask for the actuator temperature readout after five minutes of walking.
  • Confirm the number of battery packs and the charger are included; spare packs are a large share of the value.
  • Ask whether the robot was ever opened or firmware-modified.

Selling

Get the most for it

  • State the trim, firmware version, and what you were told about licence transfer.
  • Record a short video of a stand, walk, and push recovery; it answers most buyer questions.
  • Count and state battery packs and cycles if the app shows them.
  • Ship batteries according to your carrier's lithium rules; these packs are large enough to need the proper label.
