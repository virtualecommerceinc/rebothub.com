Roomba 960

A ten-year-old camera-mapping Roomba that still cleans. Parts are cheap and plentiful, but its used value is low enough that most repairs beyond a battery are not worth paying someone else to do.

Verdict key: Fix when the part costs clearly less than the robot's used value and the job is routine. Sell for parts when the fault is a board, camera or sensor on a robot whose used value is low. Buying used

Inspect it in ten minutes

  • Pay parts money only; a 960 that needs anything beyond a battery is not a bargain.
  • Confirm it starts, runs for five minutes, and returns to the dock.
  • Check the cliff sensor windows underneath for dirt and scratches.

The general inspection guide · The clean handover

Selling

Get the most for it

  • Be upfront about age and runtime; buyers of a 960 are looking for a cheap second robot for one room.
  • Remove from your account and factory reset.
  • A 960 with a fresh battery and clean filter sells faster than one without; a battery costs less than the price difference in most markets.
