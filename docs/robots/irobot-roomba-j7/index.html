Roomba j7 and j7+

The camera-navigating Roomba that promised to dodge pet accidents. Still common, still fixable, and its value now depends on what iRobot's new owner does with the app.

Verdict key: Fix when the part costs clearly less than the robot's used value and the job is routine. Sell for parts when the fault is a board, camera or sensor on a robot whose used value is low. Replace when the robot is old enough that even a successful repair leaves you with something not worth much. Get a price first when it depends on a quote or the robot's used value. Buying used

Inspect it in ten minutes

  • Ask the seller to run it from the dock for ten minutes in front of you; watch for an early return.
  • Open the app on the seller's phone and watch them remove the robot from their account. Do not accept a factory reset alone.
  • Lift the bin and check the two metal contacts for corrosion.
  • Check the Clean Base bag and the evacuation port for a packed clog.
  • Confirm the dock, power supply, and at least one spare bag are included.

Selling

Get the most for it

  • Run a full clean cycle, then empty and wash the bin and filter so the photos show a clean robot.
  • Remove the robot from your iRobot account in the app before handover, then factory reset.
  • Photograph the underside, the bin contacts, and the charging contacts; buyers of j7s ask about all three.
  • State the battery runtime you actually observed and the date you observed it.
  • Ship with the battery installed and the robot powered off; lithium batteries inside equipment ship more easily than loose packs. Check your carrier's rules.
