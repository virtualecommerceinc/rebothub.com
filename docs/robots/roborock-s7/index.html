Verdict key: Fix when the part costs clearly less than the robot's used value and the job is routine. Sell for parts when the fault is a board, camera or sensor on a robot whose used value is low. Replace when the robot is old enough that even a successful repair leaves you with something not worth much. Get a price first when it depends on a quote or the robot's used value. Buying used

Inspect it in ten minutes

  • Start it off the dock and listen: the lidar turret should spin up silently within seconds.
  • Ask the seller to delete the robot from their Roborock app in front of you.
  • Look at the water tank seal and the mop plate clip; both wear and both are cheap but tell you how it was treated.
  • Run it over a dark rug edge for a minute to prove the cliff sensors are not confused.
  • Confirm the dock type: standard, Auto-Empty, or Empty Wash Fill. They are not interchangeable.

Selling

Get the most for it

  • Clean the lidar window and the cliff sensors; the first five minutes of a buyer's test decide the sale.
  • Delete the robot from your app and reset Wi-Fi.
  • State which dock is included and whether the mop module is present.
  • Drain the water tank completely before shipping.
  • Ship with the battery installed and the robot off; tape the bumper so it cannot be pressed in transit.
