Verdict key: Fix when the part costs clearly less than the robot's used value and the job is routine. Sell for parts when the fault is a board, camera or sensor on a robot whose used value is low. Replace when the robot is old enough that even a successful repair leaves you with something not worth much. Get a price first when it depends on a quote or the robot's used value. Part cost is a tier, not a quote; prices change and we do not print them.

Buying used

Inspect it in ten minutes

  • Press CLEAN with the robot off the dock; it should start within a few seconds and run without an immediate error.
  • Watch the seller remove it from their account in the iRobot app.
  • Spin both drive wheels by hand; they should turn freely with slight resistance and spring back when pressed.
  • Look under the brush guard for hair wound into the bearings.
  • If a Clean Base is included, make sure it powers on and the bag door closes.

Selling

Get the most for it

  • Replace the filter and side brush if they are visibly worn; it costs little and lifts the price.
  • Remove from your account, factory reset, and say so in the listing.
  • List the exact runtime you observed on a fresh charge.
  • Include the dock and power supply; a robot without a dock sells for parts money.
  • Pack the robot upright in its original foam if you kept it, or wrap the bumper so it cannot be pressed in transit.
