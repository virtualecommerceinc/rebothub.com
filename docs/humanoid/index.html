      The humanoid track

      Before the first used humanoid
      is listed.

      Humanoids you can actually order today are developer and research machines: Unitree's G1 and R1 lines, and 1X's NEO on preorder for homes. Within a few years the first ones will be resold. The questions that will decide their value are being answered right now in the Unitree Go2 market, so that is where we start.

      What will decide a used humanoid's value

      • The licence, not the body. Unitree already sells Air, Pro and EDU trims that differ mainly in what software the owner may run. Whether that licence transfers to a second owner is the single biggest question, and today the answer is "ask the manufacturer".
      • Actuators and cycles. Legged robots wear their joints the way cars wear engines. Expect the first honest listings to quote walking hours and actuator temperatures.
      • Battery packs. Spare packs are a large share of a legged robot's value and ship under strict lithium rules.
      • Firmware and support. A robot whose firmware the maker no longer updates, or whose cloud features need an account the seller controls, is worth less than it looks.
      • Fall history. The humanoid equivalent of an accident report.
