Roomba after iRobot

What the Chapter 11 and the sale to Picea mean for the Roomba you own, the one you are thinking of buying used, and what it is worth.

What happened

iRobot filed for Chapter 11 protection on 14 December 2025 and emerged on 23 January 2026 as a subsidiary of Shenzhen Picea Robotics, its long-time contract manufacturer, which converted its claims into ownership. iRobot said at the time that the process would not disrupt the app, customer programmes or product support. Those assurances covered the bankruptcy period; what happens next is Picea's decision.

What it means for a Roomba you own

  • Every Roomba we cover still cleans with the CLEAN button if the app or cloud is unavailable. Maps, schedules and room-by-room cleaning depend on the app.
  • Parts remain widely available from third parties regardless of the company's status; batteries, filters, brushes, bins and wheel modules are commodity items.
  • Keep your app account in good order and note your robot's firmware version; if a change is announced you will want to know where you started.

What it means if you are buying one used

  • Pay for the hardware, not the app features. A j7 whose value rests on obstacle avoidance and smart maps is more exposed than an i7 or 960 that most people run with one button.
  • Do the account handover in person; see the clean handover.
  • Check the current app status in the store reviews the week you buy.

What it means for value

We have not yet collected enough observations to say how the news moved used prices. Every Roomba hub shows "collecting" until we have at least three dated sales. If you sold or bought one since December 2025, report it; this is the single most useful thing a reader can do for the index right now.
