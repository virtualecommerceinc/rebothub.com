Buying a used robot: the ten-minute inspection that catches most lemons

You found a used robot at a good price and want to know what to check.

Editorial guidance, not a repairRisk: low

General guidance. Model hubs carry the specific checks for each robot.

Drafted with AI assistance from the sources named above. Not yet reviewed by Dan Herlehy; it will carry a review date when it has been.

? Verdict: Get a price firstRun it, watch the account removal, check the battery and the contacts, confirm the dock. Walk away from anything the seller will not demonstrate.

Steps

  1. Ask for the exact model number from the label, not the product family name.
  2. Have the seller start it from the dock and let it run for ten minutes while you talk. Early return or an error code is your answer.
  3. Watch the seller remove it from their account in the app, then pair it to yours before money changes hands.
  4. Flip it: press both wheels, spin the brushes, look at the charging contacts and the sensor windows.
  5. Confirm the dock powers on and every accessory in the listing is present.
  6. Compare the price against sold observations, not asking prices, and against the cost of the first repair it will need.
