The clean handover: accounts, maps and personal data when a robot changes hands

You are buying or selling a connected robot and want nothing left behind.

Editorial guidance, not a repairRisk: low

General guidance. Each manufacturer's unlink and reset sequence is different; follow the official steps for your exact model and app version.

Drafted with AI assistance from the sources named above. Not yet reviewed by Dan Herlehy; it will carry a review date when it has been.

? Verdict: Get a price firstDo this on every sale, in person if you can. A robot that is still tied to the old owner's account is a returned robot.

Steps

  1. Seller: in the app, remove or delete the robot from your account before you factory reset. A reset alone does not always release the robot from the cloud account.
  2. Seller: delete saved maps, schedules and room names. On camera-navigation robots, check whether the app stores obstacle images and delete those too.
  3. Seller: note what transfers. Some accessories, subscriptions and extended warranties stay with the original purchaser.
  4. Buyer: before paying, watch the seller remove the robot from their account. Then add it to yours while you are both there. If it will not pair, do not complete the sale.
  5. Both: keep account passwords, full serial numbers and home layouts out of listing photos and messages.
  6. Both: write down the model, what was included, known faults and what was agreed. Each keeps a copy.

Questions

The seller says a factory reset is enough.

On some brands it is not. The robot can reset locally while the cloud account still lists it as the seller's device, which blocks you from pairing. Insist on seeing it removed in the app.
